# Editorial policy

Make the work understandable to a reader evaluating the candidate for the target
role. Show the contribution, why it mattered, and the judgment it required.
Use concrete work to demonstrate strengths.

## Select and compose

Choose each bullet's main contribution before drafting it. Give it one dominant
accomplishment. Select details that explain its significance.

Make the consequence visible. Explain what became possible, what changed in a
workflow, or how a system became easier to operate, diagnose, recover, or
maintain. Identify affected users or operators when that clarifies the result.
A concrete qualitative change can carry the accomplishment.

Show engineering judgment through consequential choices. Include the problem or
constraint and the decision, diagnosis, or design response when the evidence
supports them. Retain technical details that explain the result or the judgment
required.

Show organizational judgment through the work's relationship to people. Look for
understanding a business process, clarifying an underspecified need, working with
others, enabling colleagues or users, and designing around operating
responsibilities. Describe those actions and consequences directly.

Connect contribution, mechanism, and consequence. Use the order that makes the
accomplishment easiest to grasp. Use direct verbs and concise sentences. Avoid
generic claims of excellence or lessons that the work itself does not explain.

Use scale and technology deliberately. Numbers may establish reach, frequency,
workload, or improvement; make their meaning clear. Include technologies when
they establish relevant experience or explain the engineering. Keep feature lists
only when their breadth matters to the accomplishment.

Compose the bullets as a set. Weigh consequence, judgment, and relevance together.
Select complementary evidence for the target role. Each bullet should add useful
information. Preserve distinct projects and ownership boundaries when combining
related material. Operational impact, engineering judgment, and organizational
judgment guide selection; they do not require one bullet each or all three in
every bullet.
